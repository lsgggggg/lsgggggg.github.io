Falsifying Our Own Model: A Prospectively Frozen Protocol for Spatio-Temporal Forecasting
第一作者（通讯） ICLR 2027 · Full Paper · Under Review

研究衔接与问题：课题组阶段之后，我们原本计划继续优化 Orion，把精度推向 state of the art，同时让模型更可解释、设计更合理。优化中遇到了瓶颈：新加入的模块和改动大多难以带来经得起严格检验的增益；补齐比较器、改用多种子之后，Orion 也不再处于领先位置。进一步分析发现，许多看似有效的改进，其实取决于比较器集合、随机种子、反复查看测试集和判定规则。在测试预测完全不变的情况下，只去掉 MetaDG 一个对手，Orion 的“最优单元格”就从 0/36 变成 12/36；12 个模型中有 6 个会在某次“去掉一个对手”后获得新的加粗。由此衍生出本文的问题：一项模型改动的收益，究竟有多少来自改动本身？

方法一：前瞻冻结合同：我们把预注册的思想落到“单个模型改动”这一层。每个假设在结果出现前写成一份合同 C = (数据与划分, 匹配对照, 唯一改动, 种子, 主指标, 判定表, 失效条件)，并用 SHA-256 哈希冻结；其中 8 份另用 OpenTimestamps 锚定到比特币区块时间，由于区块时间有约两小时误差，其中 5 份早于首个结果超过两小时。判定由确定性 adjudicator 脚本完成，共 8 步：绑定合同摘要并核对时间先后；定位每个种子的对照与处理 run；做零效应或构造等价检查；读取逐种子主指标与守卫指标；缺任何一项就停止、不给结论；按判定表裁决；审计比较器覆盖与测试接触；输出包含合同、指标、检查、哈希与回执的证据包。测试接触账本记录了 71 个逻辑事件。

方法二：判定规则及其实测行为：对越低越好的指标，三种子门槛要求 Δ = m对照 − m处理 ≥ max(s对照, s处理)。我们没有默认它可靠，而是实测它的行为：在 18 个同配置真实种子的 A/A 零效应分区中，越线率为 10.4%（正态模型参考值 10.6%）；检出 1 个和 2 个标准差效应的概率约为 44% 和 82%。作为对照，直接“加粗更低均值”在零效应下有 50% 的比较会偏向处理组。另做了正对照：在冻结合同下反转两个已知有害的 PEMS08 归一化选择，验证 MAE 分别降低 0.927 和 0.175，都落在预设区间内。负对照包括训练数据比例实验和合成 SEIR 流行病实验。

Research Transition & Question: After the research-group stage, we set out to keep improving Orion toward state-of-the-art accuracy and a more interpretable, principled design. Optimization hit a bottleneck: newly added modules and changes rarely produced gains that survived rigorous checks, and once the comparator set was completed and runs used multiple seeds, Orion was no longer in the lead. Further analysis showed that many apparent improvements depended on the comparator set, random seeds, repeated test inspection, and the decision rule. With test predictions unchanged, omitting a single opponent, MetaDG, moves Orion from 0/36 to 12/36 best cells, and 6 of 12 models gain new highlights after some single omission. This led to the paper’s question: how much of a model change’s reported gain comes from the change itself?

Method 1: Prospectively Frozen Contracts: We apply preregistration principles at the level of a single model change. Before results exist, each hypothesis is written as a contract C = (data and split, matched control, sole change, seeds, primary metric, decision table, invalidation conditions) and frozen with a SHA-256 hash; eight contracts are also anchored to Bitcoin block times through OpenTimestamps, and because block times carry about two hours of uncertainty, five of them precede their first results by more than two hours. A deterministic adjudicator script decides in eight steps: bind the contract digest and verify chronology; locate control and treatment runs for every seed; run a zero-effect or construction-equivalence check; read per-seed primary and guard metrics; stop without a verdict if anything is missing; apply the decision table; audit comparator coverage and test exposure; and output an evidence bundle of contract, metrics, checks, hashes, and receipts. An exposure ledger records 71 logical test-access events.

Method 2: The Decision Rule and Its Measured Behavior: For lower-is-better metrics, the three-seed gate requires Δ = mcontrol − mtreatment ≥ max(scontrol, streatment). Rather than assume this rule is reliable, we measured it: across A/A zero-effect partitions of 18 real same-configuration seeds it crossed 10.4% of the time (10.6% under a normal model), and it detects one- and two-SD effects about 44% and 82% of the time. By contrast, simply bolding the lower mean favors the treatment in 50% of zero-effect comparisons. As positive controls, reversing two known-harmful PEMS08 normalization choices under a frozen contract lowered validation MAE by 0.927 and 0.175, both within prespecified bands; negative controls included a training-fraction experiment and a synthetic SEIR epidemic experiment.

Audit Findings: Applied to 48 frozen hypotheses about Orion itself, the protocol produced 15 gate-passed, 11 stopped, 9 refuted, 8 flat or inconclusive, 2 failed, 2 superseded, and 1 withdrawn outcome; 7 of the 11 stops consumed no validation, test, or GPU resources, and none opened the test set. We diagnosed a contraction bias in Orion’s forecasts and designed one observational probe and three interventions against it (a p-norm masked loss, earlier graph activation, and a five-quantile output head); none met its frozen criterion. The gains that held up were three recipe changes that passed again on fresh three-seed cohorts: PEMS08 node-identity block dropout (p=0.35; validation MAE improved by 0.130 against a same-configuration A/A shift of 0.061, and a separately frozen 18-seed-per-arm study estimated 0.107 with a 95% interval of [0.087, 0.127]), the METR-LA graph learning-rate ratio (0.01505 against a threshold of 0.00648), and PEMS-BAY graph activation (0.01903 against 0.00231). Complete Reproduction Ledger: On PEMS08, METR-LA, and PEMS-BAY, we reproduced 11 models (AGCRN, AutoSTF-NoNAS, D2STGNN, Graph-WaveNet, HimNet, MetaDG, PGCRN, ST-SSDL, STAEformer, STID, and TrendGCN) under one data split, scaling, and mask, and compared them with Orion over 12 models × 36 test cells. The Orion Model: This version of Orion removes the LLM retrieval and the causal framing in favor of a testable predictive-propagation structure. A node-local encoder projects flow, a validity mask, and two calendar channels into 32-dimensional tokens and passes them through four conditioned dilated blocks with dilation rates 1, 2, 4, and 8; the condition vector combines 64-dimensional node identity with 32-dimensional time-of-day and day-of-week embeddings, and during training the node-identity part is dropped as a block with probability p (the block dropout that passed the gate above). A directed influence module produces, for each candidate edge s→r and each sample, an edge gate g, a direction weight d, and weights γ over three lags (5, 10, and 15 minutes); lagged messages are aggregated with in-degree normalization, optionally over a second hop, and mixed per forecast step. The output is Ŷ = F(S) + Direct(C) + Igraph·κ·[F(S+M) − F(S)], where κ∈(−1,1) is learned per receiver and horizon; when the graph switch Igraph is off, the correction is exactly zero, so graph-on versus graph-off isolates the graph’s contribution. All recipes were selected on validation data only: PEMS08 uses width 128, p=0.35, and graph off; METR-LA and PEMS-BAY use width 64 and p=0.10, turn the graph on at epoch 31, and train the graph at 0.1 times the main learning rate. Compared with the CIKM version, it is more correct, more complete, and more interpretable.

研究问题：竞赛版的注意力图和主流时空模型一样，只刻画两个节点“相关”，没有方向。现实中的影响往往是单向的：论文用 METR-LA 的事故场景说明，A 点发生事故后，拥堵沿 A→B→C 向上游传播，下游的 D 始终畅通；流行病中，交通枢纽县向外围县输出病例也远比反方向容易。论文归纳了现有方法的四个不足：分不清影响的来源和去向；依赖结构是静态的；时间尺度单一；没有方法检验学到的图结构是否与它的预测作用一致。

整体框架：输入经嵌入层和正弦位置编码后，分成小时、日、周三条并行的 Belt Block 分支。每个 Belt Block 依次做 TE-CausGAT 空间注意力、多头时间注意力和前馈网络，带残差连接与 LayerNorm。融合模块对三个分支自适应加权并做注意力融合，最后线性投影输出 12 步预测。

TE-CausGAT：时变有向依赖：这是本阶段的核心模块，“因果”按 Granger 意义使用，即 X 的历史能帮助预测 Y。计算分四步：先用 1 到 15 的 8 种奇数卷积核做多尺度时间卷积；再把序列分成若干时间段，每段由独立估计器学习源节点和目标节点的变换，得到有向依赖矩阵 C(s) = σ(S(s)T(s)⊤ + B)；各段按注意力权重加总后，与高斯核构造的先验邻接矩阵做先验引导融合，Cfused = (1−α)·Σs wsC(s) + α·Cprior；最后用 GRU 模拟扰动在网络上随时间的传播，h(t) = GRU(CfusedX(t), h(t−1))。为了让有向图不出现环，用 λ2tr(Ĉ2) + λ3tr(Ĉ3) 惩罚二元环和三元环，代替计算量为 O(N3) 的矩阵指数约束。

干预式一致性校验：训练时，每个 batch 随机选 K 个节点（交通数据 30 个，流行病与 DREAM-3 为 25 个），用 v = tanh(g(c)) 生成干预值替换它们的输入并切断入边，模拟 do 算子，再经 GRU 传播，观察下游节点的预测变化。如果学到的边权 Cij 与“遮掉节点 i 之后节点 j 的预测变化”一致，validity 分数 Vi→j = 1 − |Cij − Δŷj/maxkΔŷk| 就高。这个分数作为训练信号，用来过滤只有相关、没有预测作用的边。校验后的 Cfused 以两种方式进入注意力：直接加到注意力分数上，并由三层 MLP 按传播效应缩放 value。

From Competition to Research: Building on the competition-stage Orion, I serve as an undergraduate research assistant on the advisor’s National Natural Science Foundation of China project on energy forecasting, responsible for general spatio-temporal neural-network modeling. This stage developed the competition model’s dynamic graph attention and multi-period input into a full paper, extended the application from traffic to epidemics, and was submitted to CIKM 2026 as “Orion: Spatiotemporal Neural Networks with Self-Validating Directed Graphs for Traffic and Epidemic Forecasting.”

Research Problem: Like mainstream spatio-temporal models, the competition version’s attention graph only captured whether two nodes were correlated, not in which direction influence flowed. Real influence is often one-way: in the paper’s METR-LA accident example, congestion spreads upstream along A→B→C after an accident at A while the downstream node D stays free-flowing; in epidemics, transit-hub counties seed peripheral counties far more readily than the reverse. The paper identified four gaps in existing methods: they cannot tell influence sources from targets, assume static dependence, model a single time scale, and never check whether a learned graph is consistent with its predictive effect.

Overall Framework: After an embedding layer and sinusoidal positional encoding, the input splits into three parallel Belt Block branches for hourly, daily, and weekly periods. Each Belt Block applies TE-CausGAT spatial attention, multi-head temporal attention, and a feed-forward network with residual connections and LayerNorm. A fusion module weights the three branches adaptively and fuses them with attention, and a linear projection outputs the 12-step forecast.

TE-CausGAT: Time-Varying Directed Dependence: This is the core module of the stage, using “causal” in the Granger sense: the past of X helps predict Y. It works in four steps. Multi-scale temporal convolutions with eight odd kernel sizes from 1 to 15 extract features. The series is split into segments, and an independent estimator per segment learns source and target transforms to produce a directed dependency matrix C(s) = σ(S(s)T(s)⊤ + B). The segment matrices are combined with attention weights and fused with a Gaussian-kernel prior adjacency, Cfused = (1−α)·Σs wsC(s) + α·Cprior. A GRU then simulates how perturbations propagate over the network, h(t) = GRU(CfusedX(t), h(t−1)). To keep the directed graph acyclic, λ2tr(Ĉ2) + λ3tr(Ĉ3) penalizes 2- and 3-cycles in place of the O(N3) matrix-exponential constraint.

Intervention-Based Consistency Validation: During training, each batch randomly selects K nodes (30 for traffic, 25 for epidemic data and DREAM-3), replaces their inputs with intervention values v = tanh(g(c)) and cuts their incoming edges to mimic the do-operator, and propagates the change through the GRU to observe downstream predictions. If a learned edge weight Cij agrees with how much node j’s prediction changes when node i is masked, the validity score Vi→j = 1 − |Cij − Δŷj/maxkΔŷk| is high. This score serves as a training signal that filters edges that are correlated but have no predictive effect. The validated Cfused enters attention in two ways: it is added to the attention scores, and a three-layer MLP scales the values by propagation effects.

Three-Stage Training & LLM Retrieval: Following curriculum learning, training runs in three stages: feature learning only (10 epochs); directed-structure learning with frozen embeddings (10 epochs); and end-to-end optimization with everything unfrozen (80 epochs). Continuing the competition paper’s idea of using a language model, this version adds optional LLM retrieval: TinyLlama-1.1B was fine-tuned on about 100,000 PEMS03 sequences (240 historical steps paired with 12-step targets in ChatML format), lowering validation perplexity from 8.3 to 2.1. The frozen model’s last-layer hidden states serve as semantic embeddings, and a similarity of 0.7 × cosine + 0.3 × Euclidean selects, for each node, the most similar historical day and week in place of the fixed “same time last week.”

Experiments: On METR-LA, PEMS04, and PEMS08 (traffic) and CA-COVID and TX-COVID (county-level hospitalizations), Orion was compared with 13 baselines: HA, VAR, DCRNN, AGCRN, DGCRN, STGCN, GWNet, ASTGCN, MTGNN, GMAN, STAEformer, CaST, and TESTAM. The paper reported an average MAE of 2.66 on METR-LA (6.60% below STAEformer) and 13.29 on PEMS08 (8.65% lower); the best 3-step result on PEMS04 (8.83% lower) but weaker long-horizon results; and MAE of 291.31 and 30.31 on CA-COVID and TX-COVID (22.70% and 37.73% below TESTAM). On DREAM-3 gene data with a ground-truth regulatory network, directed-structure discovery reached an AUC of 0.6295, above the strongest baseline, CUTS (0.5915).

Ablations & Checks: On PEMS08, single-stage training raised MAE by 18.36%, removing prior-guided fusion by 19.79%, single-scale convolution by 8.05%, and removing intervention validation by 3.82%. TE-CausGAT mattered more as the horizon grew: removing it was slightly better at 3 steps but raised MAE by 2.63% at 12 steps, indicating that short-term forecasts rely mainly on temporal autocorrelation and long-term forecasts more on spatial propagation. METR-LA Case Study: The directed dependency matrices change over the day. The morning peak shows a one-to-many tree: node 4 at the I-10/I-405 interchange reaches an out-degree of 20, with influence extending about 3.5 miles along northbound I-405. 参赛论文：《基于 Orion 模型的交通流量大数据建模与预测研究》。三人团队，我担任队长。这是 Orion 的第一个版本，后来的课题组研究和 ICLR 论文都从这里出发。

问题：交通流量同时受路网上的空间传播和多种周期规律影响。论文指出当时主流模型的两类不足：一是长时距预测误差会累积，精度和稳定性不够；二是过度依赖通用注意力框架，缺少针对交通场景的结构设计，计算开销也大。我们的目标是做一个同时利用近期波动、日规律、周规律和路网空间依赖的直接多步预测模型。

数据与预处理：使用五个数据集：PeMS03（358 个检测器、26,208 个 5 分钟时间步、流量）、PeMS04（307 个、16,992 步、流量/占用率/速度）、PeMS08（170 个、17,856 步、三个特征）、NYCBike2(r)（200 个区域、11,952 个 30 分钟时间步、租车量/还车量与时间索引）和 NYCTaxi(r)（200 个区域、11,952 步、双向流量与时间索引），(r) 表示随机抽样子集。处理流程为：先生成有效值掩码，使损失只在真实观测上计算；沿时间轴线性插值填补零值与缺失值；构建基于 KNN 的加权邻接矩阵，即把边距离标准化后，每个节点只保留 K 个最近邻，权重取距离倒数 1/(cost+ε) 并归一化；再用多周期滑动检索生成周、日、小时三组输入，按 6:2:2 划分并做标准化。

三周期输入：受 ASTGCN 启发，模型同时读入三段历史：预测时刻之前最近的 T 步，捕捉事故、天气等造成的突变；前一天同一时段之前的 T 步，反映通勤高峰等日规律；前一周同一星期、同一时段之前的 T 步，反映工作日与周末的差异。ASTGCN 直接截取与预测目标完全同一时段的数据，我们则统一提取对应时刻之前一小时的数据，引入时间偏移，降低模型对特定时段的依赖和过拟合风险。

时空模块：三段输入分别进入堆叠两层的时空模块，每层依次是动态图注意力、时序多头自注意力和前馈网络，均带残差连接、LayerNorm 和 Dropout。动态图注意力把路网先验和数据学到的后验结合起来：对每对节点拼接特征，用可学习向量加 LeakyReLU 打分（GAT 的做法），用 KNN 邻接掩码屏蔽不相邻的节点对，再把 KNN 先验权重加到注意力分数上后做 Softmax。先验权重在每次前向传播后被约束在初始值的 ±50% 以内并重新归一化，既借助路网结构加快收敛，又允许模型按数据调整。时序多头自注意力在每个节点上沿时间维做缩放点积注意力，建模长距离时间依赖。

周期融合与输出：多头自注意力融合模块先用线性层把每个周期分支的时间维压缩为 1，把三段拼接后分两路处理：一路用多头自注意力学习周期之间的交互权重，例如工作日早晨更看重日周期、节假日前夜更看重周周期，再扩展到预测长度；另一路做线性投影降维。两路经残差与 LayerNorm 合并，最后由前馈网络和线性层一次性输出未来 12 步。每个注意力模块都可以导出权重热力图，用来解释空间、时间和周期三方面的依赖。

训练与调参：使用 Adam 优化器和 MAE 损失，训练 50 个 epoch，输入 12 步、预测 12 步。以 PeMS04 为基准，对 8 个超参数（KNN 邻居数、模块层数、学习率、空间/时间/融合注意力头数、Dropout、前馈维度）做了 12 组实验，在精度和训练时间之间选定 K=3、2 层、学习率 1e-4、空间与时间各 8 头、融合 4 头：K 增至 5 会引入噪声，计算量增加 48.2%；减到 1 层 MAE 恶化 3.4–4.1；学习率 0.01 出现梯度爆炸；头数翻倍使训练时间增加 132.2%，精度只略有改善。

结果：与 LSTM、STGCN、DCRNN、ASTGCN、Graph-WaveNet、STID 六个基线（均调到与 Orion 相当的参数规模）比较 12 步平均误差：Orion 在五个数据集上 RMSE 全部最低，MAE 在四个数据集上最低。例如 PeMS03 的 MAE 为 15.64、RMSE 为 23.21，最强基线 STID 为 17.27、27.50；PeMS08 的 MAE 为 15.18、RMSE 为 21.98，ASTGCN 为 17.06、26.70。NYCBike2(r) 的 MAE 和 MAPE 仍是 ASTGCN 更好。随预测步长增大，Orion 的误差增长最缓。

北京应用与可解释分析：把模型用于 TaxiBJ（北京 128 个区域、14,163 个 30 分钟时间步，特征为入流量、归一化温度、风速和天气索引），Orion 的 MAE 34.51、RMSE 59.78 均为最低。读取注意力热力图：空间上，节点 56 与距离较近的节点 90 权重约 0.016，高于与较远节点 65 的约 0.008；时间上，约 8:30 和 8:50 两个时间步的权重最高；周期融合中，周周期（0.375）和日周期（0.325）的权重高于近期数据。据此提出按周规律调整信号灯配时、提前调整可变车道方向、在弱关联区域开通公交专线等建议。

不足与后续：论文在展望中指出三点不足：短时距预测的优势不如长时距明显；模型无法利用事故、新闻等非结构化信息，可以尝试借助大语言模型；一次性输出全部 12 步，限制了长序列预测的稳定性。这些问题成为课题组阶段的起点，其中借助大语言模型的设想，后来演变为 CIKM 版中的 TinyLlama 语义检索模块。

Competition Paper: “Traffic-Flow Big Data Modeling and Forecasting Based on the Orion Model.” I led a three-person team. This is the first version of Orion; the later research-group study and the ICLR paper both started here.

Problem: Traffic flow is shaped both by spatial propagation over the road network and by several periodic patterns. The paper identified two weaknesses of mainstream models at the time: errors accumulate over long horizons, limiting accuracy and stability; and heavy reliance on generic attention leaves little traffic-specific structure while adding computational cost. The goal was a direct multi-step forecaster that uses recent fluctuations, daily and weekly regularities, and spatial dependence on the road network together.

Data & Preprocessing: Five datasets were used: PeMS03 (358 detectors, 26,208 five-minute steps, flow), PeMS04 (307, 16,992 steps, flow/occupancy/speed), PeMS08 (170, 17,856 steps, three features), NYCBike2(r) (200 regions, 11,952 thirty-minute steps, pick-ups/returns with time indices), and NYCTaxi(r) (200 regions, 11,952 steps, bidirectional flow with time indices), where (r) marks a randomly sampled subset. The pipeline builds a valid-value mask so the loss is computed only on real observations; fills zeros and missing values by linear interpolation along time; constructs a KNN-weighted adjacency matrix by standardizing edge distances, keeping each node’s K nearest neighbors, weighting them by inverse distance 1/(cost+ε), and normalizing; and then generates weekly, daily, and hourly inputs by multi-period sliding retrieval, with a 6:2:2 split and standardization.

Three-Period Input: Inspired by ASTGCN, the model reads three histories at once: the most recent T steps before the forecast time, capturing sudden changes from incidents or weather; the T steps before the same time on the previous day, reflecting daily patterns such as commuting peaks; and the T steps before the same time and weekday in the previous week, reflecting weekday–weekend differences. Whereas ASTGCN takes windows aligned exactly with the target period, we consistently take the hour before the corresponding time. This time offset reduces dependence on a specific period and the risk of overfitting.

Spatio-Temporal Blocks: Each of the three inputs passes through two stacked spatio-temporal blocks, each consisting of dynamic graph attention, temporal multi-head self-attention, and a feed-forward network with residual connections, LayerNorm, and dropout. The dynamic graph attention combines the road-network prior with what is learned from data: it concatenates the features of each node pair, scores them with a learnable vector and LeakyReLU as in GAT, masks non-adjacent pairs with the KNN adjacency, and adds the KNN prior weights to the attention scores before the softmax. After every forward pass, the prior weights are constrained to within ±50% of their initial values and renormalized, so the road structure speeds up convergence while the model can still adapt to the data. The temporal self-attention applies scaled dot-product attention along time at each node to model long-range temporal dependence.

Period Fusion & Output: The multi-head self-attention fusion module first compresses each period branch’s time dimension to one with a linear layer, concatenates the three branches, and processes them along two paths. One path learns cross-period interaction weights with multi-head self-attention, for example weighting the daily period more on weekday mornings and the weekly period more on the eve of holidays, and expands the result to the forecast length; the other applies a linear projection for dimensionality reduction. The two paths are merged with a residual connection and LayerNorm, and a feed-forward network and linear layer output all 12 future steps at once. Every attention module can export weight heatmaps that explain spatial, temporal, and cross-period dependence.

Training & Tuning: Training used Adam and an MAE loss for 50 epochs, with 12 input steps and 12 output steps. On PeMS04, twelve experiments over eight hyperparameters (KNN neighbors, number of blocks, learning rate, spatial/temporal/fusion attention heads, dropout, and feed-forward width) selected K=3, two blocks, a learning rate of 1e-4, eight spatial and eight temporal heads, and four fusion heads as the best balance of accuracy and training time: raising K to 5 added noise and 48.2% more computation; one block worsened MAE by 3.4–4.1; a learning rate of 0.01 caused gradient explosion; and doubling the heads increased training time by 132.2% for only a slight gain.

Results: Against six baselines tuned to a comparable parameter scale (LSTM, STGCN, DCRNN, ASTGCN, Graph-WaveNet, and STID), Orion achieved the lowest 12-step average RMSE on all five datasets and the lowest MAE on four. For example, on PeMS03 it reached MAE 15.64 and RMSE 23.21 versus 17.27 and 27.50 for STID, the strongest baseline; on PeMS08 it reached 15.18 and 21.98 versus 17.06 and 26.70 for ASTGCN. ASTGCN remained better on NYCBike2(r) MAE and MAPE. Orion’s error also grew most slowly as the horizon increased.

Beijing Application & Interpretability: Applied to TaxiBJ (128 regions in Beijing, 14,163 thirty-minute steps, with inflow, normalized temperature, wind speed, and a weather index), Orion achieved the lowest MAE (34.51) and RMSE (59.78). Its attention heatmaps show that node 56 attends about 0.016 to the nearby node 90 but only about 0.008 to the more distant node 65; that the time steps around 8:30 and 8:50 receive the highest temporal weights; and that the fusion module weights the weekly (0.375) and daily (0.325) periods above recent data. These readings led to policy suggestions such as adjusting signal timing to weekly patterns, switching reversible lanes in advance, and opening bus lanes between weakly linked areas.

Limitations & Next Stage: The paper’s outlook named three weaknesses: the short-horizon advantage was smaller than the long-horizon one; the model could not use unstructured information such as incidents and news, for which large language models were suggested; and producing all 12 steps at once limited long-sequence stability. These became the starting point of the research-group stage, and the idea of using a language model later grew into the TinyLlama semantic-retrieval module of the CIKM version.
